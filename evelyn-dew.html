Evelyn Dew

Skin and hair care made in India: three cartons and a five-page shop where the only colour is the product’s own.

Overview

Evelyn Dew arrived with three formulas and one front panel for a sunscreen. It left with three full cartons, a shop in black and white, and a Figma file rebuilt from the site’s own stylesheet.

The brief

Turn a sunscreen front panel into packaging for the whole range, then a website that sells three products without shouting.

The client

A new care brand from India with a sunscreen, an anti-dandruff scalp serum and a hair growth oil, made by a contract manufacturer in Ghaziabad.

My part

The carton copy and every panel, a packaging studio that renders them, the five pages of the site, and a plugin that rebuilds the site in Figma.

The label came first.

Before any screen, I read 19 cartons already on the shelf, then checked every line of the prototype against the formula the team sent. Their data held 33 problems across the three products, 14 of them on the sunscreen. Switch between the two panels to see what changed and why.

  1. 1

    Hero ingredients from the real formula. The line names the mineral filter, the brightener and the humectant the team’s formula is built on.

  2. 2

    A format word. Every reference carton says what it is: gel, spray, cream. “Cream” sits under the name, set lighter.

  3. 3

    Only the numbers the data supports. The two prototype panels disagreed on PA+++ and PA++++, and the data read “SPF 50 +++”. The carton says SPF 50 PA+++ until the lab report confirms more.

  4. 4

    No claim the formula can break. Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide can leave a white cast, so “No White Cast” came off.

  5. 5

    Tests before badges. “Dermatologically tested” needs a patch test report. It stays off until there is one.

  6. 6

    The legal quantity. The tube holds 100 g, not 50 ml. The data said “100gm”, and “gm” is not a legal unit.

Two words, one drop.

The mark is a drop drawn from two letters: a small e in the upper left, a d filling the right. On the site’s Home and About pages it draws itself and circles each letter as you scroll. Pick a word to see where it lives in the mark.

Three decisions that shaped the shop.

Black and white, so the product is the colour.

Every photograph and the hero film are graded to black and white, and the page itself is paper and ink. The only colour on any screen is a product’s gradient, so you can tell the three apart from across the room.

Each product page takes one accent from its gradient for its buttons, tabs and markers: a graphite for the sunscreen, a violet for the serum, an amber for the oil.

One file for the carton and the page.

All three products live in one data file: name, actives, claims, quantity, carton size, gradient and accent. The packaging studio draws every panel and dieline from it, and the website builds its shop, product pages and ingredient guide from the same file.

So a claim corrected once is corrected on the carton, the product page and the science page together.

The label is the content.

A new brand has no reviews yet, so the site leans on what is printed. The science page walks through each hero ingredient, what it is, what it does and which product it is in, and every product page carries the full ingredient list as it appears on the carton.

The copy only repeats what the cartons claim. Nothing new is promised online.

The site, rebuilt in Figma from its own CSS.

Instead of drawing the design system by hand, I wrote a Figma plugin that reads the stylesheet, the images and the product file and builds the whole file in about a minute. When the site changes, the file is rebuilt, so the two never drift apart.

How it came together.

From the project files, in order. Packaging first, then the site, then the system.

Packaging26 and 27 October

  1. 26 Oct

    Nineteen reference cartons and the prototype panel read and written up: what every front and back panel carries, in what order, and what the law asks for.

  2. 26 Oct

    Carton copy written for all three products from the team’s data, with a list of the problems found in it and the questions only the manufacturer can answer.

  3. 27 Oct

    A packaging studio that draws every panel, dieline, pack and the lineup from one product file, and renders them to images.

Website and system4 to 6 November

  1. 4 Nov

    The brand’s final logo files and gradient artwork arrived. The site uses them; the carton renders from 27 October still carry the first gradients.

  2. 4 Nov

    Five pages built: home, shop, product, science and about, with a bag drawer, a newsletter form and the black and white film.

  3. 5 Nov

    The Figma plugin written: tokens to variables, 23 component sets, every page at desktop and phone size, the prototype wired.

  4. 6 Nov

    The website committed to its own repository, then published at evelyndew.com.

Where it stands

The site is live at evelyndew.com. The photographs on it are licensed stock in black and white, and the people in them have nothing to do with the brand.

The cartons are written and drawn, and wait on the manufacturer: the marketer’s address, the lab reports and the printer’s dieline.
