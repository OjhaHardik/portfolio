Evelyn Dew

Skin and hair care made in India: a five-page shop in black and white, where the only colour is the product’s own.

Visit evelyndew.com See the screens
Client
Evelyn Dew, a sunscreen, a scalp serum and a hair oil
Role
UI and UX, front end, Figma design system
Built with
HTML, CSS, JavaScript, GSAP, Lenis, the Figma plugin API

Overview

A new brand with three products and no reviews yet needed a shop that feels trustworthy from the first screen. It got five pages in black and white, where each product’s own colour does the selling, and a Figma file rebuilt from the site’s own stylesheet.

The brief

A website that sells three products without shouting, and tells you plainly what is in each one.

The client

A new care brand from India with a sunscreen, an anti-dandruff scalp serum and a hair growth oil, made by a contract manufacturer in Ghaziabad.

My part

The UI and UX of all five pages, the front end, and a plugin that rebuilds the site in Figma.

Two words, one drop.

The mark is a drop drawn from two letters: a small e in the upper left, a d filling the right. On the site’s Home and About pages it draws itself and circles each letter as you scroll. Pick a word to see where it lives in the mark.

Evelyn. Beauty that is desired. The skin you want to wake up with.

Dew. The freshness and hydration of morning dew. Light, clean and quiet.

Three decisions that shaped the shop.

Black and white, so the product is the colour.

Every photograph and the hero film are graded to black and white, and the page itself is paper and ink. The only colour on any screen is a product’s gradient, so you can tell the three apart from across the room.

Each product page takes one accent from its gradient for its buttons, tabs and markers: a graphite for the sunscreen, a violet for the serum, an amber for the oil.

One product file, nine places on the site.

All three products live in one data file: name, actives, claims, size, gradient and accent. The home page index and ritual cards, the shop grid and its slider, each product page and the two products suggested under it, and the science page’s actives, ingredient guide and free-from list are all built from it.

So adding a product, or correcting a claim, is one edit instead of nine.

The label is the content.

A new brand has no reviews yet, so the site leans on what is on the pack. The science page walks through each hero ingredient, what it is, what it does and which product it is in, and every product page carries the full ingredient list.

The copy only repeats what the product label claims. Nothing new is promised online.

The site, rebuilt in Figma from its own CSS.

Instead of drawing the design system by hand, I wrote a Figma plugin that reads the stylesheet, the images and the product file and builds the whole file in about a minute. When the site changes, the file is rebuilt, so the two never drift apart.

Five pages, in black and white.

Each screen is one whole section of the live build at 1440 px, then four views at 390. The photographs are stock stand-ins, graded the way the real shoot will be.

Where it stands

The site is live at evelyndew.com. The photographs on it are licensed stock in black and white, and the people in them have nothing to do with the brand.

The Figma file is rebuilt from the same stylesheet, so a designer can pick it up without the two drifting apart.
