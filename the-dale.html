The Dale

A boutique farmstay on the quiet side of Kumaon, with a brand and a site built to make the quiet believable.

How the mark was made
Client
The Dale, ten rooms on a working farm at Rausil
Role
Brand, UI and UX design, front end
Built with
HTML, CSS, JavaScript, GSAP, Lenis

Overview

The Dale is ten rooms among mango orchards at Rausil, 12 km from Kathgodam station. Every stay in the hills says it is peaceful, so the site had one job: make the quiet believable before anyone has been.

The brief

Make the quiet believable, then make it easy to ask for a room, a retreat or the whole estate, without a booking engine in the way.

The guest

People who have done Nainital once and did not enjoy the crowd, and groups who want the lawn, the hall and the pond to themselves.

My part

The brand first: the mark, colour and type. Then the one-page site: the copy structure, the design and the front end.

Prove the quiet by contrast.

The first thing after the hero is a table: Nainital in season on one side, The Dale on the other, five times of day. No adjective has to do any work.

Switch sides. Every line is the site’s own.

Want to see Nainital anyway? It’s an easy day trip. Then come back to the quiet.

Rooms that open like doors.

There are four ways to stay: two valley-view cottages, six orchard rooms, a garden suite, or the whole estate. Each is a narrow panel; one opens wide with its photographs and the rest fold to strips.

Rates are marked “on request”, and one line under the row lists what every room has, so no card repeats it.

Say how busy it is.

Every place near the estate carries an honest tag, from Peaceful to Very busy. Nainital and Kainchi Dham are on the list, with “go early on a weekday”, because a guest who gets stuck in traffic stops trusting the rest of the page.

The ashram a short walk from the gate is written about as a morning walk, never as an advertisement.

Ask the way people already talk.

The enquiry starts with what you are planning: a stay, a retreat, a wedding or event, the whole estate. Name, WhatsApp number and arrival date are the only required fields, and errors appear beside the field, not in a pop-up.

Rates come back on WhatsApp, which is where the family already answers.

Shot on the estate, not generated.

The family’s own photographs carry the page, and the footer says so. Where one is still a stand-in, it says that too.

Brand guidelines.

The photographs are green and grey, so the colour does very little: a cool off-white like cloud, the dark green of a deodar for every action, and a brick red kept for the odd line that needs it.

Voice

Speak like a good host.

Plain, warm and specific. Name the real thing: the dish, the month, the tree. The brand sheet keeps two lists.

The page, end to end.

One long page, desktop and phone side by side, in the order a guest meets it.

Where it stands

The page is designed, built and reviewed, and waits for its domain. The enquiry form validates and says thank you, but the last step, sending it to email and WhatsApp, is not wired yet.

What I’d do next

Connect the form
Send each enquiry to the family’s email and WhatsApp, with a copy to the guest.
Real rates
“On request” is honest for now. A seasonal range would help the people who just want a number.
Notes from the farm
A short monthly page of what is ready on the slope, so the site is different in July from December.

A quiet place needs a site that doesn’t shout.
