Bakhoor
Al Barkaah

A one-product shop for a hand-blended bakhoor, sold through the moments it is burned for.

Visit the shop
Client
Bakhoor Al Barkaah, a hand-blended bakhoor
Role
UI and UX design, front end, back end
Built with
HTML, CSS, JavaScript, PHP, MySQL, Razorpay

Overview

Bakhoor is wood and resin, warmed on a coal until the smoke rises. Nobody can smell it through a phone, and there is only one blend to sell, so the shop had to sell the moment instead of the notes.

The brief

One blend, a green box and a gold calligraphy mark. It needed a shop that takes an order and a payment, and an admin that prints a GST invoice.

The customer

Families who burn bakhoor before prayer, on Fridays and for guests, and people buying it as a gift, mostly from a link shared on a phone.

My part

The shop and its admin: design, front end, PHP and MySQL, Razorpay, the order emails and the printed invoice.

Sell the moment, not the notes.

An ingredient list doesn’t smell of anything. So the home page names five times the bowl is lit, each with a photograph of it already in that room and one line from the shop.

One row, one button.

The row scrolls sideways and a single arrow steps along it. Order now sits beside the heading, not under every card, so the photographs stay photographs.

Let the history vouch for it.

A new brand has no reviews to lean on, so the page borrows something older. One quotation from Sahih Muslim stands alone in the middle of the page, and three short stories follow: The Incense Road, A Quiet Sunnah, and Given, Rarely Bought.

They explain why bakhoor matters before anyone is asked to buy it, and they make it a gift as much as a purchase.

One product, one page to order it.

The order page holds the product, a quantity stepper with a live total, and a shipping form. There is no cart screen and no account to make. Fields are underlined, not boxed, so the form looks written rather than filled.

Behind the single button the server checks stock again before it creates the order. Stock only drops once the payment is verified, and the confirmation email goes out right after.

Brand guidelines.

The box is deep green and the mark is gold, so the shop keeps to those two and lets the warm photographs do the rest.

Logo

A calligraphy emblem and one spaced line.

“Bakhoor Al Barkaah” written in Arabic, inside a round seal. On the box and in the footer it sits over the name in spaced capitals. In the shop’s top bar it stands alone.

Motion

The mark, drawn in twelve seconds.

A short film for reels and stories. The seal writes itself in gold on white, then the name settles underneath.

Logo film, 12 s, 1080 by 1920

It plays here only while it is on screen. On a phone it is the first thing a follower sees of the brand, so the mark arrives before the product does.

Components

Square, thin and slow.

No rounded corners anywhere in the shop. Hover any of these: they behave the way they do on the live site.

−1+

The dark button fills with gold on hover. The light one, used only over the hero photograph, lifts three pixels. Fields are a single forest line that turns gold when you type.

Most people arrive on a phone.

A scent is shared by message, so every page was checked at 390 px. The hero swaps to a portrait photograph, the occasion row becomes a swipe, and the order form stacks with the button at the end.

Behind the counter

A private admin lets the owner edit the product and its stock, see every order, and print the invoice. A Payments and GST report totals each month and the financial year, and the invoice splits tax into CGST and SGST, or IGST, depending on the buyer’s state.

Until the payment keys were added, the whole flow ran in a demo mode with a simulated payment and emails written to a log, so the owner could test it end to end without a real charge.

What I’d add next

A gift note
The stories call bakhoor something you give. A “send as a gift” option with a printed card would finish that thought.
A second blend
The order page is built for one product. A second blend would need a small range page, but not a catalogue.
Delivery estimate
A pincode check before payment, so nobody finds out about delivery time afterwards.
