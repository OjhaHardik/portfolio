Aafreenn

A made-to-order store for handmade Indian suits, cut to the buyer’s height.

Visit aafreenn.in
Client
Aafreenn, a small label for farshi, gharara and shalwar sets
Role
UI and UX design, front end, back end
Built with
HTML, CSS, JavaScript, PHP, MySQL, Razorpay

Overview

Aafreenn sold every suit through Instagram and WhatsApp: a photo, a price, a long thread about fit, then a bank transfer and a screenshot for a receipt. The store had to answer the fit question before anyone asked it.

The brief

A store as calm as the photographs, that answers fit before anyone has to ask, takes payment, and can be run by the owner from a phone.

The customer

Women ordering a suit made to their size, who mostly arrive from the link in the label’s Instagram bio, on a phone.

My part

Reading the order chats with the owner, the design system, the store and its admin, from the first layout to the payment webhook.

Every thread asked the same four things.

I read back through a few months of order chats with the owner. The questions repeated so closely that they became the outline for the product page.

  1. “Will it fit me?”

    Size and height are picked together, right where you add to cart. Each suit is cut to the height you choose, from 4′11″ to 5′11″, and the size chart sits one tap below.

  2. “Do you still have it?”

    Nothing is cut until it’s ordered, so each design has one stock count shared by every size. Low stock reads “Hurry, only 5 left!” Sold-out designs stay in the grid, marked, so the range still looks full.

  3. “When will it reach me?”

    Dispatch time and the returns policy sit in an accordion under the buy buttons, before checkout, not in an email after it.

  4. “Can I just message you?”

    Yes. A WhatsApp button stays on the shop, product, cart and contact pages. For a label this size, talking is part of buying.

From the first photo to paid.

Home

One photograph and a door.

The home page is a single full-screen photo, an Enter button and the tagline “For moments worthy of praise” running along the bottom. No banners, no carousel. The clothes make the first impression.

Shop

The whole range on one page.

Farshi, gharara and winter sets in a four-column grid. Filter by availability, collection or price, sort by newest or price. Each card flips through its photos without opening the product.

Quick view

Buy without leaving the grid.

Choose opens a quick view with the same size and height controls as the product page. Someone who already knows what they want never has to change page.

Product

Size, height, done.

Photos on the left, decisions on the right, in the order you make them. The size chart, specifications and shipping live in accordions below, there when you need them.

Cart

Every line keeps its own fit.

Size and height are stored per item, not per order, so one cart can hold a suit for you and another for your sister. Shipping is free, so the total never surprises.

Checkout

Pay as a guest.

No account needed. If the email already has one, a short prompt offers to continue without a password. Payment opens Razorpay, so UPI, cards and netbanking all work.

    Components

    Choices are boxes, moves are pills.

    The rule that holds the store together, rebuilt here in HTML at the real sizes.

    SMLXL Select your height -1+ Add to cart Proceed to checkout

    A size is a square box with a 2 px maroon border, filled when chosen. Anything that moves you towards paying is a pill.

    Most visits start on a phone.

    Every page was checked at 390 px: a two-column grid, filters stacked above it, size buttons big enough for a thumb, and a WhatsApp button that never covers the price.

    Behind the counter

    A separate admin lets the owner add designs with several photos, manage collections, move orders through their stages and see every email the store has sent.

    Each status change emails the customer, so nobody has to ask where their order is.

    What I’d add next

    Fit memory
    Remember a returning customer’s size and height, so the second order is two taps.
    Collection pages
    A lookbook per collection once the range grows past one screen of designs.
    Cash on delivery
    For first orders, behind a small prepaid deposit, since many buyers here still prefer it.
